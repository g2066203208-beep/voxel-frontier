Steel-concrete hybrid wind turbine tower                  associated with traditional steel towers, making it increasingly popular in onshore low-wind-speed wind farms.

                                                          structures. The vibration governing equation for the hybrid tower was derived using the principle of virtual

Rotor-nacelle assembly (RNA)
                                                          displacement and the generalized coordinates method, considering the mass eccentricity and moment of inertia
                                                          of the rotor-nacelle assembly, variable cross-sections of the tower, and prestress. An adaptive vibration mode for
                                                          the hybrid tower was constructed, and an intelligent optimization algorithm was employed to solve the natural
                                                          frequencies. A refined finite element model was developed using ABAQUS software to predict the natural fre­
                                                          quencies and vibration modes of the hybrid tower, validating the reliability of the intelligent analysis approach.
                                                          Moreover, the impact of different simplified rotor-nacelle assembly models on the natural frequencies of the
                                                          hybrid tower was explored, revealing that models neglecting moments of inertia overestimate structural natural
                                                          frequencies, particularly for higher modes. Finally, the feature importance of various geometric dimensions of
                                                          the tower was assessed employing a machine learning model, and parametric analysis of the geometric di­
                                                          mensions was conducted. The results show that tower height, proportion of concrete tower segment height, and
                                                          bottom diameter of concrete tower segment are critical factors influencing the natural frequencies.

4.2. Validation

    To verify the reliability of theoretical solutions for the hybrid tower’s
dynamic characteristics, the first three natural frequencies and vibration
modes obtained from FE results were compared with theoretical calcu­
lations. The comparison of the first three natural frequencies is shown in
Table 3, and the corresponding vibration modes are illustrated in Fig. 6.
As seen, for the first three natural frequencies and vibration modes of the
hybrid tower, the theoretical solutions agree well with the FE results,
thereby validating the accuracy of the proposed method for analyzing
the dynamic characteristics of hybrid towers based on adaptive vibra­
tion modes. The theoretical results for the first-order frequencies along
the u and v directions closely agree with the FE results, with relative
errors within 5 %, meeting engineering design requirements. Besides,
due to the complexity of higher-order vibration modes, lower modes
exhibit higher agreement than higher modes.
