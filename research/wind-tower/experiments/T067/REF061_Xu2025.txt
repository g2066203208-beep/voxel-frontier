ARTICLE               INFO                               ABSTRACT

Keywords:                                                The Onshore Wind Turbine (OWT) with a Steel–Concrete Hybrid Tower (SCHT) exhibits unique dynamic
Steel–concrete hybrid tower                              characteristics due to its combination of a steel upper section and a concrete lower section, distinguishing
Co-simulation                                            it from traditional steel towers. This study proposes a co-simulation approach to develop an integrated,
Multi-body dynamics
                                                         fully-coupled model of OWTs with SCHT, combining multi-body dynamics and finite-element methods for
Onshore wind turbines
                                                         nonlinear dynamic response analysis. First, the multi-flexible body system of the OWT is divided into the
Time history analysis
                                                         blade system and the tower system, with a force–force coupling method applied to manage the interactions
                                                         between these components. The equation of motion for the blade system is derived using the Euler–Lagrange
                                                         equations, establishing a multi-body dynamic model. This allows for the detailed simulation of blade dynamics,
                                                         capturing their flexible behavior and interactions with other turbine components. Then, a finite-element model
                                                         employing beam elements is developed with OpenSEES software to describe the structural behavior of the
                                                         SCHT, incorporating material nonlinearity. This model captures the distinct structural responses of the steel
                                                         and concrete sections. The interactions between these two separate systems are then integrated using an
                                                         iterative coupling method to formulate the co-simulation approach. This iterative method ensures accurate
                                                         representation of the dynamic interactions between the two subsystems, leading to a more realistic simulation
                                                         of the overall turbine behavior. Numerical investigations are conducted to verify the effectiveness of the
                                                         proposed co-simulation model. Additionally, both linear and nonlinear dynamic response analyses of OWTs
                                                         with SCHT under extreme wind conditions are performed.

                                                                                      representation method. For the latter, the Kaimal spectrum is utilized
                                                                                      as the reference spectrum [31].

3. Co-simulation model for wind turbines with steel–concrete hy-
brid tower

    In a multi-body system, different components often exhibit varying
levels of structural complexity. Employing a uniform analysis method
across the entire system can result in inaccurate results or high com-
putational costs. Therefore, it is advantageous to decompose structures
of varying complexities into relatively independent components. These
components can be analyzed separately and their individual solutions                             Fig. 5. Simplified diagram of CS model.

integrated afterward, reducing computational costs and enhancing ac-
curacy. This method is referred to as the CS approach [36]. Consid-
ering the complexity of the SCHT section and its material behavior,
along with the relatively simple characteristics of the blades and hub,
a co-simulation model will be established to develop an integrated,
fully-coupled model for the SCHT wind turbine.

solver.                                                                                       Fig. 14. Model overview and connection detail in Abaqus.
    Fig. 14 illustrates the FE model of the SCHT developed using
Abaqus software. This model employs three-dimensional elements, with
embedded truss elements inside the concrete tower acting as rein-
forcement mesh. Additionally, the model includes rebar elements to
simulate prestressed steel reinforcement extending the entire length of
the concrete tower from top to bottom. The constitutive behavior of the
concrete is represented by a damage plastic model [51], while the steel
mesh, tower segments, and prestressed reinforcement are described by
bilinear plastic constitutive models. A lumped mass at the top of the
model represents the weight of components such as the blades and
nacelle, and fixed constraints are applied to both the tower’s foundation
and the bottom of the prestressed rebar [52]. As depicted in the detailed
illustration in Fig. 14, the connection between the steel and concrete
towers uses a flange joint, with the top end of the prestressed steel rebar
also fixed to this flange.
