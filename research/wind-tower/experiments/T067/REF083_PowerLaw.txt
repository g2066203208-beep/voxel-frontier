                                                      found that, onshore, the global median of the absolute percentage error related

                                                      Assuming a constant value of 0.14, the power law is less accurate (absolute per-

                                                      results provide a comprehensive global overview of the errors to be expected
                                                      from using the mean of the power law exponent or assuming a value of 0.14.
                                                      In many regions where the wind resource is abundant, using the mean of the
                                                      power law exponent only leads to minor errors in capacity factor estimation.
                                                      There, the assessment of wind resources with small errors is possible, even in
                                                      the absence of long-term wind speed measurements at different heights.

2.3 | Analysis of the temporal variability
of the power law exponent

The temporal variability of E was assessed at seasonal
and inter-annual scales. The seasonal behavior of E was
evaluated for (a) December, January, February (DJF),
(b) March, April, May (MAM), (c) June, July, August
(JJA), and (4) September, October, November (SON). EM
was calculated for each season yielding EMDJF, EMMAM,
EMJJA, and EMSON.
    The investigation of the inter-annual variability of
E is based on the annual mean values of E for the entire
investigation period. Both, the seasonal and inter-annual
variability of E were quantified by the standard deviation
(sd) and the range (r).
