Analysing the uncertainties of reanalysis data used for wind resource
assessment: A critical review
G. Gualtieri
National Research Council–Institute for BioEconomy (CNR-IBE), Via Caproni 8, 50145, Firenze, Italy

                                                             The results confirmed that reanalyses can predict wind resource pattern over time, while they are not similarly
Height                                                    able over space. Reanalysis data (particularly by ERA5) are sufficiently reliable on offshore and flat onshore
                                                          locations. Uncertainties are greater on mountainous and coastal sites, where wind speed and energy yield are
                                                          significantly under- and over-estimated, respectively. On these locations, the use of higher-resolution regional
                                                          products should be preferred over global datasets as they are better suited to resolving detailed terrain features as
                                                          well as roughness and topographical discontinuities. Comparison between older and newer reanalysis versions
                                                          revealed that the latter do not always outperform the former. In many cases, however, improvements by the
                                                          newer products were found – rather than in accuracy itself – in pushing a given accuracy towards higher ele­
                                                          vations, thus more suitably meeting the needs of modern wind power industry. In perspective, it is desirable that
                                                          advances in forecasting models will enable current problems in assimilating surface land wind observations by
                                                          global reanalyses to be overcome. Refinements in global datasets could also positively reflect on regional
                                                          products.

aimed at selecting only those studies exactly matching the scope of the
review. This has required establishing a set of specific criteria to be met.
As detailed in Table 2, these criteria focus on those articles solely dealing
with (global or regional) reanalysis products, thus ignoring, e.g., anal­
ysis, forecasting, or NWP models. Only applications that use reanalysis
data as direct input have been considered, so that, e.g., model coupling/
downscaling (use of reanalysis as initial and/or boundary conditions),
statistical manipulation, or bias correction have been discarded. As a
reference for comparison, only observed data (e.g. from met stations,
met masts, vertical profilers, buoys, lightships) have been considered,
being also collected from point monitors or at most from wind farms,
thus excluding spatial aggregation to clusters of wind farms, regions, or
even countries. A minimum timespan of ~1 year [1] with a time reso­
lution not higher than 6 h has been considered.
    To increase their accuracy – without using model downscaling –,
reanalysis data are generally spatially interpolated from original grid
points to the target location(s) using methods such as nearest neighbour,
bilinear interpolation, inverse distance weighting, etc. [65]. Likewise, to
match their height for comparison, either reanalysis or observation data
may be vertically interpolated or extrapolated using models such as the
logarithmic or power law (e.g. Ref. [45]). As for these adjustment pro­
cedures performed in the literature, no filter has been applied here.
    The review is limited to those articles that validate the reanalysis
products through at least one variable between mean wind speed and
AEY (or alternatively CF), all calculated as overall (i.e. not sub-yearly)
values. At least one of the following metrics should be available: MB,
RMSE, or R for wind speed; plus PE for AEY. R was not considered for the
latter due to the very limited availability of applications in the literature.
For convenience, when MB is not available, this value is approximated
