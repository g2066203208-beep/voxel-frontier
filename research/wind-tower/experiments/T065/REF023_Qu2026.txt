Fatigue Life Evaluation of a Steel–Prestressed Concrete Hybrid
Tower Under Prestress Relaxation Using a Bidirectionally
Coupled Damage Model and Neural Network Surrogate
Chang Qu, Pengyong Miao *, Chengyun Li, Ziyue Li and Yanru Wu *

                                         School of Civil Engineering and Smart Construction, Chang’an University, Xi’an 710064, China
                                         * Correspondence: p.y.miao@outlook.com (P.M.); wuyr@chd.edu.cn (Y.W.)

                                         Abstract
                                         This study investigates the long-term fatigue performance of an in-service 4.55 MW
                                         steel–prestressed concrete hybrid wind turbine tower under different tendon relaxation
                                         scenarios. The fatigue lives of prestressing tendons and concrete are calculated from finite
                                         element stress responses obtained in ABAQUS. A bidirectionally coupled damage model
                                         then updates the damage and stiffness of the two materials to determine the overall cou-
                                         pled fatigue life. Based on these results, a Spatial-Topology and Physics-Informed Cascade
                                         Network (STPI-Net) is developed for life prediction. The results show that fatigue life
                                         reductions vary across tendon relaxation cases and generally increase with prestress loss.
                                         Windward-side and simultaneous multi-tendon relaxation cause larger reductions, and
                                         damage coupling further reduces overall fatigue life in all studied cases. At 80% prestress
                                         loss, the overall coupled fatigue life reaches 0.45 years in the severe three-tendon relaxation
                                         case, representing a 28.6% reduction relative to uncoupled life. Scenario-based five-fold
                                         cross-validation is further used to evaluate the predictive performance of STPI-Net. The
                                         R2 values are 0.8879 for logarithmic tendon fatigue life, 0.9030 for logarithmic concrete
                                         fatigue life, and 0.8195 for overall coupled fatigue life. The proposed framework supports
                                         the assessment of component and overall fatigue performance under different prestress
                                         relaxation conditions.

                                         Keywords: steel–concrete hybrid tower; prestress relaxation; fatigue life evaluation;
                                         bidirectional damage coupling; physics-informed neural network

                                 In recent years, substantial research has been conducted on the structural performance
                           and fatigue durability of wind turbine support structures. Alonso-Martinez et al. [1] and
                           Seidel et al. [2] examined the structural behavior of bolted flange connections in wind
                           turbine towers, demonstrating the necessity of considering nonlinear effects and geometric
                           imperfections in connection analysis. Zhang et al. [3] investigated the mechanical perfor-
                           mance of curved steel–concrete–steel composite walls for wind turbine towers, highlighting
                           the role of bidirectionally coupled damage in structural behavior.
                                 The fatigue performance of wind turbine towers is also closely related to the evolution
                           of material damage. In concrete, performance degradation is usually related to stiffness
                           degradation, crack propagation, and damage accumulation. Riyar et al. [4] and Wei et al. [5]
                           review concrete fatigue behavior and related fatigue analysis methods. Their work covered
                           fatigue crack growth, fracture processes, size effects, and life prediction methods based
                           on S–N curves, fracture mechanics, and damage mechanics. Qin et al. [6] extended the
                           microplane model to steel-fibre-reinforced concrete and linked fatigue history variables to
                           stiffness degradation, indicating that stiffness loss can characterize progressive concrete
                           fatigue damage. Saqif and El-Tawil [7] developed an elastoplastic damage constitutive
                           model for UHPC using cyclic compression test data, revealing the progressive stiffness
                           and strength degradation of concrete under repeated loading. Other studies have focused
                           on the evaluation of concrete degradation. Hassan et al. [8] combined non-destructive
                           testing measurements with a mix-specific regression model to predict concrete compressive
                           strength and emphasized the importance of material-specific calibration. Imoh et al. [9]
                           investigated concrete containing waste glass cullet and snail shell powder and showed
                           that changes in material composition significantly affected its workability and mechanical
                           strength. Studies on geomaterials have also provided insights into internal structural
                           evolution and fracture development linked to macroscopic mechanical degradation [10,11].
                                 Fatigue evolution is further affected by load characteristics, damage accumulation,
                           and structural degradation. Therefore, researchers have improved and extended the
                           traditional Palmgren–Miner linear cumulative damage rule. Sadeghi et al. [12] proposed
                           a rainflow residual reconstruction method to compensate for lost low-frequency large-
                           amplitude fatigue cycles caused by 10 min signal truncation for offshore wind supports.
                           Haselbozchaloe et al. [13] indicated that nonlinear damage models consider load-order
                           effects and critical-plane schemes handle non-proportional loads. Zhu et al. [14] developed
                           a Markov-subset simulation fatigue reliability model, showing that long-term operation
                           without inspection greatly raises failure probability. In addition to fatigue damage caused
                           by loads, the effects of environmental degradation have also received increasing attention.
                           Studies have shown that corrosion degradation and welding residual stress can accelerate
                           fatigue deterioration by affecting structural conditions and crack propagation [15,16].
                                 The high computational cost of fatigue analysis has also encouraged the develop-
                           ment of data-driven surrogate models for the efficient prediction and assessment of wind
                           turbine structures. Bai et al. [17] developed a residual neural network using hub-height
                           wind speed statistics to reduce the need for repeated numerical simulations. Similarly,
                           Santos et al. [18] incorporated fatigue-related physical information into a physics-guided
                           neural network, achieving errors below 3% for long-term accumulated damage-equivalent
                           moment estimation. Their results demonstrated the value of physical information in data-
                           driven fatigue prediction. Some other studies have combined continuous-time Bayesian
                           networks and Bayesian optimization to assess wind turbine blade fatigue reliability and
                           predict material fatigue life [19,20]. These methods integrate finite element simulation, ex-
                           perimental data, and parameter uncertainty, improving prediction reliability. Data-driven
                           methods also enhance real-time operations and maintenance (O&M). Studies [21–23] lever-
                           age SCADA data alongside Gaussian process regression, health index modeling, and neural



                                                                                  https://doi.org/10.3390/buildings16193854

                           networks for turbine condition assessment and anomaly detection. For physical damage
                           inspection, Li et al. [24] combined UAV imagery with an attention-enhanced grid CNN
                           for autonomous concrete tower crack inspection. In addition, recent work scales these
                           methodologies from single components to entire wind farms. Pacheco et al. [25] used
                           two years of strain-monitoring data to develop a probabilistic farm-wide fatigue estima-
                           tion framework, showing that monitoring information from selected turbines can support
                           fatigue assessment across a wind farm.
                                 Although existing studies have made considerable progress in fatigue damage mod-
                           eling and data-driven life prediction for wind turbine structures, several issues remain
                           unresolved. In particular, the prestress degradation effect of steel–prestressed concrete
                           hybrid wind turbine towers has not been systematically investigated. The fatigue response
                           of these towers is affected by the interaction among tendon relaxation, concrete damage
                           evolution, and external loading. Stress redistribution caused by prestress loss may further
                           change local fatigue development and overall fatigue life. However, the effects of different
                           relaxation locations, relaxation modes, and prestress loss levels on the fatigue performance
                           of hybrid towers remain unclear. The relationship between local component degradation
                           and overall fatigue life also requires further investigation. In addition, an effective method
                           for incorporating prestress loss information into fatigue life prediction has not yet been
                           fully established for the condition assessment of in-service wind turbine towers.
                                 To address the above issues, this study investigates the fatigue performance evolution
                           of an in-service 4.55 MW steel–prestressed concrete hybrid wind turbine tower under
                           different tendon relaxation cases. An integrated analysis framework is established that
                           combines high-fidelity finite element simulation, fatigue damage assessment, bidirectional
                           damage coupling analysis, and data-driven prediction. First, a refined three-dimensional
                           finite element model of the hybrid tower is established in ABAQUS. Different tendon
                           relaxation cases are then constructed to examine the effects of relaxation location and
                           prestress loss level on fatigue performance. This paper mainly displays the cases of single-
                           tendon relaxation (SR1–SR5) and multiple-tendon relaxation (MR1–MR4) under different
                           prestress loss levels (20%, 40%, 60%, and 80%). These cases can reveal the influence of
                           typical spatial degradation modes on structural performance. Based on the fatigue analysis
                           results, a bidirectionally coupled damage model is established to describe the interaction
                           between prestressing tendon and concrete. This model is used to describe the effects of
                           stiffness degradation and stress redistribution on overall fatigue performance. The resulting
                           component and overall fatigue life data are then used to develop a Spatial-Topology and
                           Physics-Informed Cascade Network (STPI-Net). The network uses the spatial configuration
                           and prestress loss characteristics of the tendons as input information to predict fatigue life at
                           critical regions and for the overall structure. The proposed integrated analysis framework
                           combines high-fidelity numerical simulation with data-driven prediction. It provides
                           an approach for fatigue performance evaluation and life prediction of steel–prestressed
                           concrete hybrid wind turbine towers under different prestress relaxation conditions.

                           2. Fatigue Analysis Methodology
                                Steel–prestressed concrete hybrid towers are subjected to alternating wind loads
                           throughout their service life, rendering the tower structure highly susceptible to fatigue-
                           related issues. Over the typical design life of 20 years, the cumulative number of stress
                           cycles experienced by the tower can reach 5.29 × 108 , placing the tower response within the
                           high-cycle fatigue regime [26]. To evaluate the fatigue life of the hybrid tower, this section
                           outlines the fundamental theories and methods employed in time-domain fatigue analysis,
                           encompassing the rainflow counting method, S–N curves, mean stress correction, and the
                           linear cumulative damage rule. The flowchart is presented in Figure 1.

                           2.1. Rainflow Counting Method
                                 The rainflow counting method [27] is used to convert each stress time history into a set
                           of fatigue cycles. In this study, the rainflow function in MATLAB R2020b is used to perform
                           the cycle counting. This function follows ASTM E1049-85 [28]. It first identifies the turning
                           points in the stress history, and then extracts closed cycles. Complete cycles are counted as
                           full cycles, while unmatched residuals are counted as half-cycles. For each identified cycle,
                           the stress range or amplitude, mean stress, and cycle count are obtained. These results are
                           subsequently used for mean-stress correction and cumulative fatigue-damage calculation.

                           3.2.3. Rotor Aerodynamic Force
                                The time-varying aerodynamic loads on the rotor are calculated using blade element
                           momentum (BEM) theory. At each time step, the local wind speed is obtained from the
                           stochastic wind field described in Section 3.2.2. The relative wind speed and inflow angle of

                           7. Deep Learning-Based Fatigue Life Assessment
                           7.1. The Proposed STPI-Net Framework
                                To address the high computational cost of conventional bidirectionally coupled dam-
                           age simulations and rapidly quantify how local component deterioration cascades to in-
                           duce global structural failure, a Spatial-Topology and Physics-Informed Cascade Network
                           (STPI-Net) is proposed for multi-scale fatigue life assessment of steel–prestressed concrete
                           hybrid towers.
                                As shown in Figure 18, the established framework operates under a dual-space col-
                           laborative prediction paradigm. Specifically, component-level degradation prediction is
                           implemented in a smoothed logarithmic fatigue space to stabilize gradient descent opti-
                           mization during multi-task joint training. Subsequently, a nonlinear inverse transformation
                           layer maps the intermediate logarithmic prediction results back to the linear physical
                           time domain.

                           tendon is determined by its loss rate, defined as αi ∈ [0, 1]. Therefore, STPI-Net converts
                           the health state of 16 prestressing tendons into a 16-dimensional spatial input vector:

                           shared features for multi-task prediction.

                           7.2. Experiments and Validations
                                All training and validation samples adopted in this study are generated by high-
                           precision finite element method (FEM) numerical simulations. The dataset covers typical
                           engineering working conditions, which are intended to represent operational damage
                           characteristics of structural systems.



                                                                                             https://doi.org/10.3390/buildings16193854
Buildings 2026, 16, 3854                                                                                              29 of 34



                                To improve the dataset’s coverage of different prestress loss patterns, this study
                           further expands the prestress relaxation scenarios. For the anchorage region containing
                           16 prestressing strands, single-strand loss conditions are first considered, comprising
                           16 spatial position combinations in total. Subsequently, cases involving the simultane-
                           ous loss of two or three strands are further considered. Adjacent strand combinations,
                           opposite-position strand combinations, and other random combinations are included.
                           These scenarios simulate local prestress degradation under different spatial distribution
                           forms. They also cover the structural response characteristics associated with the simulta-
                           neous degradation of multiple strands.
                                Considering different strand loss forms, a total of 69 different prestress loss topology
                           combinations are generated. Combined with four prestress loss levels of 20%, 40%, 60%,
                           and 80%, 276 independent damage scenarios are ultimately formed for the training and
                           validation of the STPI-Net model. This expanded dataset can cover structural degradation
                           patterns under different spatial topology forms and loss degrees, enabling the model to
                           learn the nonlinear relationship between strand spatial distribution, prestress loss level,
                           and fatigue life degradation.
                                This study adopts a scenario-based five-fold cross-validation strategy. Specifically,
                           working conditions with the same spatial loss topology characteristics are grouped into the
                           same data group and kept intact during data partitioning. In each fold of validation, the
                           validation set contains complete damage scenarios that are not involved in model training.
                           Therefore, model evaluation not only reflects its fitting ability to known working conditions
                           but also examines its prediction ability for unknown prestress loss combinations.
                                For each cross-validation fold, the mean µtrain and standard deviation σtrain are calcu-
                           lated using only its training subset. The same values are then used to standardize both the
                           training and held-out validation subsets according to Equation (51):

                                In terms of the validation, the coefficient of determination (R2 ) and mean absolute
                           error (MAE) are selected as quantitative evaluation indices to comprehensively assess the
                           out-of-sample prediction performance of STPI-Net across all cross-validation folds.

                           ent backpropagation during multi-task training, and accurately characterizes the highly
                           nonlinear fatigue deterioration of concrete under coupled wind loads and prestress loss.
                           Minor deviations of individual sample points are mainly attributed to extreme prestress

                           8. Engineering Implications and Limitations
                           8.1. Engineering Implications
                                The proposed fatigue assessment framework provides a basis for fatigue assessment
                           and condition monitoring of steel–prestressed concrete hybrid wind turbine towers. The
                           results indicate that windward-side tendons, adjacent tendon groups, and the middle and
                           lower concrete regions are more sensitive to prestress relaxation. These regions may there-
                           fore be prioritized in tendon monitoring, fatigue inspection, and maintenance planning.
                           For the investigated tower, 20% prestress loss can be used as a preliminary warning level
                           because investigated cases with prestress loss reaching 20% result in fatigue life shorter
                           than the 20-year design life. The spatial distribution and magnitude of prestress loss
                           obtained from monitoring data can be used as inputs to STPI-Net for estimating compo-
                           nent and overall fatigue life, thereby linking prestress-loss identification with structural
                           fatigue assessment.

                           8.2. Limitations and Future Research
                                The fatigue life of a hybrid wind turbine tower is affected by its material properties,
                           structural configuration, and site-specific wind loading. Therefore, the results of this
                           study should be interpreted mainly as fatigue-life trends under the investigated prestress-
                           loss cases. Application to other tower types requires appropriate parameter updating
                           and verification.
                                The study mainly considers tendon relaxation as the source of prestress loss. In
                           practice, prestress loss may also result from concrete shrinkage and creep, among other
                           long-term mechanisms. The 60% and 80% prestress-loss cases are introduced as severe
                           parametric scenarios to examine sensitivity and identify critical degradation states. They
                           are useful for evaluating the progressive reduction in fatigue performance under high loss
                           levels, although their quantitative results should be interpreted within the assumptions
                           of the adopted structural model. Future studies should consider combined prestress-loss
                           mechanisms and further examine the structural response under severe degradation.
                                The STPI-Net training and validation data are mainly generated from finite element
                           simulations. Experimental and field-monitoring data can be introduced to calibrate the
                           model, account for structural and loading variability, and further improve its generalization
                           and reliability.

                           9. Conclusions
                                This study established a numerical and analytical framework to evaluate the long-
                           term fatigue performance of a steel–prestressed concrete hybrid wind turbine tower under
                           different tendon relaxation cases. By integrating a novel stiffness-feedback bidirectional
                           damage model with a deep learning surrogate, the fatigue performance of the structure
                           was investigated. The main conclusions are as follows:
                           (1) Prestress relaxation reduces the fatigue performance of both prestressing tendons
                               and concrete. At 80% prestress loss, the minimum tendon fatigue life decreases to
                               11.48 years under windward-side single-tendon relaxation and to 4.66 years under
                               concentrated multi-tendon relaxation. OR5 and OR6 remain the critical concrete
                               regions, with the minimum concrete fatigue life decreasing to 0.31 years under severe
                               multi-tendon relaxation. These results show that fatigue performance depends not
                               only on the level of prestress loss, but also on its location and spatial distribution.
                               These findings highlight the need to prioritize windward-side regions and adjacent
                               tendon groups in fatigue monitoring and inspection.
                           (2) The bidirectionally coupled damage model captures a progressive feedback effect
                               that further reduces the overall fatigue life of the tower. Concrete stiffness degrada-


                                                                                  https://doi.org/10.3390/buildings16193854
Buildings 2026, 16, 3854                                                                                                          32 of 34



                                        tion increases the stress demand in the tendons, while tendon prestress loss reduces
                                        concrete pre-compression and accelerates concrete damage. As a result, the coupled
                                        fatigue life falls below the 20-year design life even at 20% prestress loss. In the MR1
                                        case at 80% prestress loss, it decreases to 0.45 years, which is 28.6% lower than the
                                        corresponding uncoupled fatigue life. These findings show that damage coupling
                                        causes an additional reduction in overall fatigue life, and this effect becomes more
                                        significant under concentrated multiple-tendon relaxation.
                                  (3)   The proposed Spatial-Topology and Physics-Informed Cascade Network (STPI-Net)
                                        incorporates spatial tendon configurations and relaxation conditions. Scenario-
                                        based five-fold cross-validation gives R2 = 0.8879 for logarithmic tendon fatigue life,
                                        R2 = 0.9030 for logarithmic concrete fatigue life, and R2 = 0.8195 for overall coupled
                                        fatigue life. The results demonstrate the potential of the proposed model for fatigue
                                        assessment based on prestress loss data.
